# Project Mode — 《门外，是四海》

This is a **project-specific director profile** for the Jinan University 120th-anniversary short-film concept 《门外，是四海》. It is not a factual source. All dates, captions, institutional names, gate identities, and historical claims still require the Evidence Ledger.

Activate when:
- the title or user request mentions `门外，是四海`;
- the user asks to continue the gate-based JNU 120 film;
- project profile is `jnu_gate`.

## 1. Core creative mandate

The film must **not** become “five gates = five slides/chapters.” The gate is a changing narrative symbol:

`physical gate -> threshold -> migration -> interruption/persistence -> rebuilding -> connection -> present choice / outward direction`

The exact number of historically defensible gates remains provisional until verified. If four gates are stronger than five, use four. If a key historical transition has no reliable gate image, represent the transition through map/document/sound rather than invent a gate.

## 2. Audience and desired after-effect

Primary audience:
- competition judges who may know the institutional history;
- JNU students/alumni who recognize the emotional stakes;
- general viewers who do not know the chronology.

Desired interpretation after one viewing:
> “This was not a campus architecture montage. I felt a school repeatedly crossing thresholds, and the people/documents/routes made the gates mean something.”

Desired recall after ten minutes:
- one strong gate/threshold image;
- one human/archive detail;
- one rupture/migration transition;
- one final transformation of what “门外，是四海” means.

## 4. Project-specific creative lab

Before rewriting the final storyboard, generate **at least 6 mechanism variants** while keeping the factual spine stable. Do not change only wording.

Required axes:

### Emotional entry
- curiosity: why so many thresholds?
- rupture/loss: what happens when a campus disappears?
- belonging: who keeps crossing the gate?
- continuity/pride: what survives when the address changes?

### Narrative mechanism
- threshold mystery
- archive investigation
- route/journey
- accumulation of people/evidence
- present student looking backward (only if production supports a real/consented person)
- object/word repetition

### Visual mechanism
- nested gates / impossible depth (clearly metaphorical composite)
- gate geometry match-cut
- archival-paper spatial corridor
- plan-line -> route-line -> gate edge
- photograph white border -> architectural frame
- archive fragments converging into one present frame
- split-time gate alignment
- sound-led blackout/rupture

Select a combination after critique, not before divergence.

## 5. First-frame laboratory — mandatory

Do not begin with a six-second slow reveal of a gate by default. Produce three first-frame propositions and compare them.

### Opening A — Nested threshold
Present-day gate is the main frame; inside its opening, an earlier verified gate image is aligned at impossible depth. Clearly a visual metaphor, not documentary evidence.

Question created:
> How can one gate contain another time?

Strength:
- instantly communicates the governing motif;
- can be built from verified images/compositing rather than invented history.

### Opening B — Archive misalignment
Present gate and historical gate are aligned in a split/matte so architecture nearly matches but one crucial geometry/time layer does not. The mismatch becomes the question.

Question created:
> What changed, and what remained?

### Opening C — Human evidence through the gate
A verified early group photograph/document appears as a spatial layer beyond the present threshold, while the gate remains physically stable. Use only if the archival people are preserved as a still image.

Question created:
> Who crossed this threshold before us?

### Opening selection rule
Judge the opening without narration first. A successful opening should let a viewer say something close to:
> “This film is about a present gate opening into different periods/people.”

Then test the first VO/question. Do not make the VO carry an incomprehensible image.

## 6. Final-frame laboratory — mandatory

The final frame must rhyme with the chosen opening.

### Ending A — Reverse temporal gaze
Return to the same axis/composition as Opening A. Instead of looking from the present into the past, the visual layers resolve so the present gate opens outward into contemporary light/space. Historical layers are now behind/within the present rather than ahead.

Meaning change:
`the gate was an entrance into history -> the gate becomes an outward-facing threshold`

### Ending B — Gates collapse into one
Several verified gate geometries briefly align in depth, then collapse/resolve into the present gate. The final image holds long enough for the viewer to feel that the motif has converged rather than merely ended.

### Ending C — Evidence leaves the frame
Documents/routes/archival layers that filled the film recede to the edges, leaving the present threshold and open space beyond it. Use sound to complete the emotional release.

Do not use a generic world map/globe unless a specific evidence/graphic strategy justifies it. “四海” should emerge from the film's human and geographical evidence, not from stock symbolism.

## 7. Provisional sequence architecture

This is a **director hypothesis**, not a locked chronology. Reorder or collapse sequences when evidence/story demands.

### SQ0 — The impossible present (0–10s)
Story job: create the visual question.
Visual engine: chosen first-frame proposition + threshold movement.
Must not: logo intro, slow gate beauty shot with no question.

### SQ1 — People before chronology (roughly 10–45s)
Story job: establish that the history is about people crossing/returning, not only buildings.
Likely evidence pool: early student/group/gate photographs.
Visual engine: archive corridor / layered evidence discovery.
Rule: do not display A089/A090/A091/A092 as four consecutive full-screen Ken Burns shots.

### SQ2 — From school to larger institution (roughly 45–80s)
Story job: expansion/change in institutional form and place, only where verified.
Likely pool: plan + gate + document.
Visual engine: plan-line -> architecture / geometry match.

### SQ3 — Rupture and route (roughly 80–115s)
Story job: the first major structural break in the film.
Visual engine: line breaks/leaves the plan -> route; sound drops before image changes.
Use absence/black/silence strategically instead of fabricated war imagery.

### SQ4 — Persistence away from the expected campus (roughly 115–165s)
Story job: prove continuity through multiple independent pieces of evidence.
Likely pool: Jianyang gate + graduation/association/protection-committee evidence.
Visual engine: archive accumulation around a stable threshold, contact-sheet space, or evidence constellation.
The evidence should accumulate into an argument, not a slideshow.

### SQ5 — Rebuilding / reopening (roughly 165–205s)
Story job: return/reconstruction is a transformation, not just another location card.
Visual engine: photo border/blank field/architectural geometry resolves into Guangzhou material; use event photo(s) as evidence of institutional restart.

### SQ6 — The meaning of the present gate (roughly 205–235s target)
Story job: answer the opening without summarizing every date again.
Visual engine: chosen final-frame rhyme; previous motifs briefly converge.
Hold the emotional payoff before credits/AI disclosure.

Target runtime for the current competition concept: **about 3:30–4:00** unless the script/evidence strongly supports another duration, while always staying under the official 5-minute ceiling.

## 9. Motion grammar for archival material

### Preferred motion sources
1. editorial cut
2. foreground occlusion
3. virtual camera through separated 2.5D layers
4. map/vector line
5. paper/document layer movement
6. light/texture modulation
7. start/end-frame transition
8. restrained environmental motion only when historically/visually defensible

### Normally forbidden
- old-photo faces blinking/smiling/talking;
- historical people walking out of group photos as if filmed live;
- flags, vehicles, crowds, weather, architecture or signage invented for “cinematic feeling”;
- elastic gate geometry;
- AI-generated Chinese signage replacing verified pixels.

Use generated movement to connect evidence, not to counterfeit evidence.

## 10. I2V prompt profile for gate shots

When an approved first frame already defines the gate/photo composition, the English video prompt should focus on motion:

```text
SHOT PURPOSE:
[what new story state this shot creates]

SUBJECT MOTION:
None. Preserve all people/architecture in the archival source exactly.

ENVIRONMENTAL / LAYER MOTION:
[foreground archival layer / paper edge / dust-free texture / map line / light shift]

CAMERA MOTION:
[one controlled movement]

TIMING:
[0-1s settle, 1-4s movement, final 0.5-1s settle]

END STATE:
[precise composition needed for the next match cut]

CONTINUITY LOCKS:
[ARCH/DOC/STYLE IDs]

DO NOT:
No face animation; no added people; no invented signage; no geometry warp; no modern objects.
```

For a hero transition, explicitly design both start and end keyframes before video generation.

## 11. Sound is part of the gate motif

Suggested designed motifs (not claimed historical audio):
- one restrained footstep pattern
- paper unfold/page movement
- hinge/threshold resonance used sparingly
- pencil/route-line sound
- room tone opening/closing
- silence at rupture

Rules:
- do not add every motif to every gate;
- a sound motif must change meaning with the image;
- use J-cuts/L-cuts so the next era can begin in sound before picture;
- clearly distinguish designed SFX from archival recordings in production notes.

## 12. Attention / retention map for a 3.5–4 minute judged film

This is not a social-ad formula. Use it as a diagnostic:

- 0–8s: visual question / first-frame contract
- by ~30–45s: first human/evidence payoff
- by ~70–90s: new scale or institutional state
- ~90–120s: rupture / prediction correction
- ~120–170s: evidence-based persistence / emotional build
- ~170–210s: reconstruction / return
- final ~20–30s: motif convergence + answer + emotional hold

If a 30-second window adds only more dates/photos but does not change the question, visual engine, or emotional state, compress/redesign it.

### F. Motion/edit/sound — 10
- movement comes from editorial purpose;

## v1.5 chaining addendum for current model limits

If the active generation model allows **max 3 reference images** and **max 10 seconds per shot**, organize 《门外，是四海》 as linked micro-shots rather than long self-contained scenes.

Recommended slot logic for adjacent continuity-sensitive shots:
1. carry-forward frame from the previous approved shot;
2. authoritative gate/document geometry reference;
3. shot-specific control frame (map crop, border-layer comp, or end target).

Use chaining especially for:
- present gate → historical gate nested passage;
- border-line / white-frame carries between archival images;
- route-line continuation from Shanghai to Jianyang;
- accumulation of A176 / A177 / A184 into an evidence wall.

Do not chain by default for:
- a shot whose previous result has geometry drift or text corruption;
- a conceptual reset where a different historical source should dominate;
- any shot that would imply fabricated human motion in archival photographs.

## v1.6 — 《门外，是四海》 automatic chain-prompt pattern

For the current max-3-reference / max-10-second workflow, each gate-film sequence should be divided into linked micro-shots and compiled with `scripts/compile_chain_prompts.py`.

Recommended slot patterns:

### Present gate → historical gate passage
- `Ref1`: approved carry frame from the present-gate shot (`CF_*`)
- `Ref2`: authoritative present/historical gate geometry reference, whichever identity must dominate the current shot
- `Ref3`: approved end-target or nested-gate composite frame

### Archival paper / border reveal
- `Ref1`: carry frame preserving the current paper-edge position
- `Ref2`: authoritative archival photo/document being revealed
- `Ref3`: composite target showing where the next archive layer should land

### Shanghai → Jianyang route continuation
- `Ref1`: prior route-line carry frame
- `Ref2`: verified map/plan geometry reference
- `Ref3`: next route/end-state composite or Jianyang entry frame

### Jianyang evidence-wall accumulation
- `Ref1`: approved previous evidence-wall state
- `Ref2`: current archival photo being added (for example A176/A177/A184)
- `Ref3`: approved composite layout/end target

For this project, prefer **composite anchors** over spending all three slots on separate archive images. The video model should animate a designed visual state, not decide the layout of four or five historical documents by itself.
